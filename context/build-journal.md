## How this file is maintained

This file grows for the life of the project and is **not** part of the session read order.
Nothing here is required to make a decision — anything that still constrains future work gets promoted to `constraints.md`, which is the file consulted during ordinary work.
That separation is what keeps the cost of knowing "what binds" from growing with the length of the build.

- **Append a dated entry after every completed feature**, under the current phase: decisions made, gotchas hit, verification results.
- **Compact at every phase checkpoint, never continuously.** When a phase closes:
  1. **Promote** anything from that phase that still binds into `constraints.md`, filed under its topic.
  2. **Collapse** the phase's per-feature entries into a handful of summary bullets.
  3. **Drop every `Verified:` line** — it has done its job once the next feature passes.
- Only the current phase keeps full per-feature detail. Earlier phases stay compacted, newest first.

Compaction is recoverable: this file is committed, so `git` history holds every detail ever removed.

## Phase 4 — Trading Engine

### Feature 24 — Order execution function  *(2026-08-22)*
- **Four defects, three of them in already-shipped code, and every one was found by hand-computing an expected figure rather than by a failing test.** Recording that because it is the pattern: each was invisible to the identities and to the CHECK constraints, and each would have shipped.
  1. **§7's short-cover cash row double-debited entry charges.** It settled from `average_price`, which is net of charges that were already debited at entry as their own `CHARGES` row, so `available_cash` ended a full round trip short by exactly them — identity 1 fails on *every* short cover, not on an edge case. The cash row now uses the gross `entry_reference_price`; `trades.realised_pnl` keeps the net average per §9. §12.11 was narrowed rather than broken: the *reported* P&L never reads the gross average, and the cover's cash row is not a P&L calculation but the settlement of proceeds that were never credited.
  2. **`reserve_margin` made covers unaffordable** (F23's function). It asked for `quantity × price` on every buy, including a buy that covers a short — so a user who shorted most of their balance could not close their own position, because that money is in `used_margin` by construction. Short 800 at 100.00 leaves 3969.92 in cash against an 80000.00 cover. Symmetric to the shorting-excess rule F23 already had on the sell side. Delivered as a **new migration**, not an edit to F23's already-applied one.
  3. **`execute_order` wrote two `CHARGES` rows on a short entry.** §6 step 6 is part of `transfer_margin_to_position`, which already releases the estimate and debits the actual; `execute_order` then wrote its own. Both rows are real cash, so identity 1 held and §12.6 held — only the arithmetic on the expected balance showed it.
  4. **The capped-loss path credited the shortfall on top of a partial debit**, leaving 2916.96 of cash where the answer is 0.00. `REALISED_PNL` now carries the loss in full and the adjustment carries exactly the uncovered part — and the credit is written **first**, because `available_cash >= 0` and `balance_after >= 0` are non-deferrable CHECKs and the other order dies with 23514 on the intermediate row.
- Decision: **`place_order` returns `(order_id, status, rejection_reason)`.** A business rejection returns normally, so `error` is null and a bare uuid leaves the Server Action unable to tell a fill from a rejection; raising instead would roll back the very REJECTED row §4 and the Orders page require. `architecture.md`'s example is corrected.
- Decision: **session logic is implemented twice on purpose.** `place_order` must reject a MARKET order outside the session and is granted to `authenticated`, so a check living in a Server Action is bypassed by anything calling the RPC. `architecture.md`'s "one place decides market time" invariant is amended to name both and to forbid a third. `market_state` mirrors the TypeScript's *method* — fixed IST offset added to the UTC instant — not merely its result.
- Decision: **a fill crossing zero apportions charges pro-rata**, closing share rounded and the remainder to the opening leg, with **one** `CHARGES` row for the whole order. §8 never covered the case; rejecting it would have made F23's shorting-excess reservation unreachable.
- **`now()` ties every row one transaction writes**, and three columns were ordered by it. `trades.traded_at` and `orders.placed_at` joined `fund_ledger.created_at` on `clock_timestamp()`. Not cosmetic: F28's matcher fills every crossed order in one run and F29's square-off closes every position in one, so Reports would render a whole square-off in arbitrary order. Found because F24's suite places several orders in one transaction and could not ask for the trade it had just written.
- **`market_state` is stubbed inside both tier-2 suites, deliberately.** A suite whose result depends on the day and hour it runs is worse than no suite — these were written on a Saturday, when every market order would be `MARKET_CLOSED`. Tier 4 proves the real function; tier 2 needs a *deterministic* session, and the stub is created inside the transaction the rollback removes.
- Gotcha: a pgTAP helper returning `record` cannot have its columns referenced — `could not identify column "status" in record data type`. It needs `returns table (...)` and a call in `FROM` position.
- Gotcha: tier 3 runs against the one real database, so the fill tests trade an **inactive** fixture symbol rather than writing a price for RELIANCE. `is_active = false` also keeps it out of the tick's demand union and F21's index composite. Cleaned up in `afterEach`, and the absence of strays verified afterwards.
- Verified: `10-orders.sql`, 76 assertions, every figure hand-computed in a comment — the CNC round trip and its weighted average, the short entry proving charges *lower* a short's average, partial and full cover, the crossing fill, all five rejection codes, the session gate, cancellation, the capped-loss case landing on exactly 0.00 with a true uncapped −15016.54 in `trades.realised_pnl`, and reset.
- Verified: `11-order-identities.sql`, 500 randomised orders across two accounts, two symbols, both products, both sides and both order types with the price walking between each, then identities 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 12. Churn asserted per *outcome* first — a run that only rejected would satisfy every identity trivially. Plus the grant list, asserted as a set rather than one function at a time.
- Verified: tier 3, two concurrent `place_order` calls that each fit and together do not produce one fill and one rejection; two concurrent `execute_order` calls on one order produce one trade, one debit, one charge. **Falsified** by removing the post-lock status guard — two trades, two debits, exactly the double-fill the guard exists to prevent. Restored.
- Verified: `tests/parity/market.parity.test.ts` over fifteen boundary instants and every minute of a trading day, **falsified** by moving the close to 15:31 in SQL only. The dense sweep is one `generate_series` query — written as 1440 round trips first, it timed out before it could find anything.

### Feature 23 — Margin reservation and release  *(2026-08-22)*
- **`trading-contract.md` §6 and §7 were wrong in four places, and each one changed code.** Planning found them; they were fixed in their own commit before anything was built, with §13's sweep run afterwards.
  1. **§6 reserved 100% of notional for a short while collateral at fill is 120% plus closing charges**, so `delta` was positive by roughly a fifth of the trade on *every* short — not merely on the gap-up fill §6 describes as the exception. That also made §7's short-entry `MARGIN_RELEASE` row a block rather than a release, and it let a user place a maximum-size short that its own fill then rejected for want of the top-up. A short now reserves the collateral formula evaluated at the reservation price; buys are unchanged. **This is the amendment with the widest blast radius**, and `08-margin.sql` asserts the consequence directly: a fill at the limit gives `delta = 0.00`.
  2. **§6 step 1 said "using the post-fill `average_price`"** — a typo for `entry_reference_price` that contradicted the formula printed three lines above it, §12.11, §12.12, and this feature's own falsification test.
  3. **§6 step 2's delta ignored collateral already held on the position**, so a fill that *adds* to a short blocked it a second time. Now subtracts `orders.blocked_margin + positions.blocked_margin`.
  4. **§7's table showed two ledger rows on a short entry where §6 steps 4 and 6 write three.** §6 won and §7 was corrected — the paired `MARGIN_RELEASE +charges` / `CHARGES −charges` rows are what make "estimated charges are never paid twice" auditable in the ledger rather than netted away inside the function.
- Decision: **`transfer_margin_to_position` takes the fill and runs *before* the trade and position rows are written.** `(p_order_id, p_fill_price, p_actual_charges)` returning `(ok, required_collateral, entry_reference_price)`. §6 step 3 can refuse a fill, and refusing at a point where nothing has been written means the caller only has to stamp `REJECTED` — the single-argument alternative would have had to read the fill back off rows that then needed a plpgsql exception block to roll them back as a subtransaction.
- Decision: **it also computes `entry_reference_price` and hands it back.** §12.11 says the two averages never cross; giving the gross average exactly one author, inside the module that also owns the collateral, makes that structural instead of a rule someone has to remember.
- Decision: **a fourth function, `recompute_position_collateral(user_id, symbol, new_net_quantity)`, owns the release side** — and takes the quantity the position is *about to become*, because the two cases that matter cannot be expressed afterwards: a full cover deletes the row (§8) and a flip to long cannot carry collateral at all (`positions_collateral_on_shorts_only`). It refuses to *increase* collateral, since an increase means an add took the release path and that fill carries a reservation only the transfer knows how to retire.
- Decision: **the §6 formula is written once**, in an `IMMUTABLE` `short_collateral_requirement`, called by all three movers. `short_margin_buffer()` sits beside it rather than inside `charge_rates()` — it is a risk parameter, not a charge — and tier 4 now compares it against `SHORT_MARGIN_BUFFER` in `constants.ts`, because F25's ticket will quote the margin a short requires from the TypeScript copy while the collateral actually held comes from the Postgres one.
- Decision: **an MIS sell crossing zero reserves on the shorting excess only.** Selling 10 against an MIS long of 4 closes 4 and shorts 6; the closing 4 carry no obligation. The contract covered neither this case nor the add case above.
- **Finding — `fund_ledger.created_at` defaulted to `now()`, which made §12.2 untestable and unenforceable.** `now()` is the transaction start time, so every row a money function writes carries the identical timestamp — and a short entry writes three. "The newest row" was a tie broken by whatever the planner returned. Changed to `clock_timestamp()`. Found only because the randomised suite had to order the ledger; no amount of reading would have surfaced it.
- Gotcha: **pgTAP files here go through `scripts/run-pgtap.mts`, not psql**, so `\set` and `:'var'` are syntax errors. Every fixture id is a literal in these two suites.
- Verified: `08-margin.sql`, 57 assertions, every expected figure hand-computed in a comment above it from the §3 rates — the CNC round trip, release idempotence, the refusal that writes nothing, the crossing-zero and fully-covered reservations, the three cash assertions on one short round trip, the zero-delta clean fill, the absence of any ledger row for the collateral amount, the exact three-row entry ledger, the add, both gap-up branches, partial and full cover, and `42501` for all six functions.
- Verified: `09-margin-identities.sql`, 500 randomised reserve/fill/cover/cancel operations across two accounts and two symbols on a **pinned seed**, then identities 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 and 12. It asserts the churn happened first — >300 orders, >100 fills, >100 releases — because a loop that quietly did nothing would otherwise pass every identity.
- **Falsified twice, both reverted.** `short_margin_buffer()` moved to 0.21: `08-margin.sql` and `test:parity` both went red, naming the figures. The delta's `positions.blocked_margin` term removed: `08-margin.sql` failed on used_margin, cash and §12.3, and the randomised suite failed identity 3 independently — which is the pair working as intended, since 08 catches the arithmetic and 09 catches the path.
- A third falsification is asserted rather than performed: `short_collateral_requirement(100, 99.94)` — the net average — is **7.20 short** of the 12005.06 a genuine 20% move plus closing charges costs, which is exactly `100 × 0.06 × 1.20`.
- Verified: `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm build`, `pnpm format:check` all 0; `pnpm test` 253 across 19 files; `pnpm test:db` 14 files; `pnpm test:parity` 5. Types regenerated.

### Feature 22 — Charge calculator  *(2026-08-22)*
- **Re-verified the §3 rate table against zerodha.com/charges first**, as §3 requires at the start of any phase that touches money. Every rate unchanged since 2026-08-21. One thing worth recording so a future re-check does not read it as drift: Zerodha prints GST as "18% on (brokerage + SEBI charges + transaction charges)" and does **not** name the DP base in it — because it folds GST on DP into the ₹15.34 DP line (₹3.50 CDSL + ₹9.50 Zerodha + ₹2.34 GST). Presentation difference, not a rate difference; the totals agree. Noted in §3.
- Decision: **a fourth test tier, read-only.** Proving the two calculators equal needs TypeScript and SQL in one process, and no existing tier can do it — tier 1 has no database, tier 2 cannot call TypeScript, tier 3 is opt-in because it commits. `calculate_charges` writes nothing, so `pnpm test:parity` needs no gate and joins `test:all`. It **fails rather than skips** without `TEST_DATABASE_URL`: a skipped tier 3 is a deferred risk, but a skipped tier 4 means the only check comparing the calculators silently did not run.
- Decision: **rates live in one `IMMUTABLE` `charge_rates()` composite.** Postgres inlines immutable SQL functions, so it costs nothing when F24 calls the calculator under a row lock, and the parity test can compare **rate for rate** rather than only inferring from totals — two rate sets can agree on the inputs a property test happens to draw and still differ.
- Decision: `calculate_charges` returns `(total numeric, breakdown jsonb)`, so F24 does `select … into` and inserts both columns with no cast on the money path. The jsonb keys were never this feature's choice — `trades_breakdown_has_all_components` pinned that exact snake_case set back in F11.
- **plpgsql, not SQL, for the calculator.** It gives up inlining to gain input guards; a charge calculator that quietly returns negative money for negative quantity is a bad thing to leave in a schema. The inlining argument applies to `charge_rates()`, called per row; the calculator is called once per fill.
- **Both carried-over Phase 1 items closed, and both falsified rather than merely passing.** The §2 reconciliation case now sums the components in **integer paise** instead of rebuilding `roundToPaise(Object.values(breakdown).reduce(…))` — that expression was the implementation restated, asserting it equals itself. Integer arithmetic owes the implementation nothing and still catches a switch to rounding the unrounded sum, which was the one thing the old version did catch. Falsified by making exactly that switch. `DP_CHARGE_INCLUSIVE` is now asserted as `roundToPaise(DP_CHARGE_BASE * (1 + GST_RATE))`; falsified by moving the base to 14.00 and watching it fail.
- **Found a real §2 discriminator rather than assuming one existed.** Most inputs give the same GST whether you take 18% of the unrounded sub-components or of the rounded ones, so an arbitrary case would have asserted nothing. Searched the input space in SQL: 2 shares at ₹41.67 (turnover 83.34) gives 0.00 under §2 and 0.01 under the wrong reading. Pinned in both tiers.
- **Exact parity, and the bar was measured before it was chosen.** Float64 versus `numeric` was the obvious risk. Before committing to exact equality I checked 600,000 random component comparisons and 6,000 *constructed* exact half-paisa midpoints — the epsilon-nudged `roundToPaise` never diverged from exact decimal half-up. Random sampling alone would not have shown this, since it almost never lands on a midpoint.
- Gotcha: `create or replace function` **preserves** the existing ACL — only a fresh `create` gets the EXECUTE-to-PUBLIC default. Checked `pg_proc.proacl` directly after the falsification round rather than trusting that, because a silently re-opened grant on a money function is exactly the kind of thing that would never surface. A future migration that DROPs and re-CREATEs these would reset them, which is what the suite's `42501` assertions are for.
- Verified: `07-charges.sql` 25 assertions — four worked examples each hand-computed in a comment above its assertion, the §2 discriminator, DP base ₹13.00 with its ₹2.34 of GST inside the single `gst` key, delivery brokerage exactly zero, the intraday cap flat at ₹20 across three magnitudes, side/product conditionality, three input guards, and `42501` for `anon` and `authenticated`.
- Verified: `pnpm test:parity` — 132 inputs (12 fixed straddling the cap boundary, 120 seeded-random) agreeing on all seven components and the total, seed printed so a failure reproduces. **Falsified** by perturbing `exchange_txn_rate` to 0.0000308 in Postgres only: both the rate-for-rate assertion and the property test failed, naming the offending inputs. Restored and re-verified.
- Verified: `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm build`, `pnpm format:check` all 0; `pnpm test` 253 across 19 files; `pnpm test:db` 12 files.
- Doc consistency: `code-standards.md`'s tier table and its "Why three" paragraph, and `CLAUDE.md`'s command list, both said three tiers. Updated together — the tier count is stated in two files and neither updates itself.

## Phase 3 — Terminal Shell & Live Prices *(compacted 2026-08-22 at the Phase 3 checkpoint)*

Features 17–21 built the signed-in terminal: shell, watchlist, live quote store, provenance, dashboard.

- **The shell owns the singletons, and pages beneath it own nothing.** The session is checked once in `(terminal)/layout.tsx`; one Realtime channel and one `requestAnimationFrame` driver are mounted there so both survive navigation. The rail lives beside `<main>` and only the sheet trigger sits in the nav — one component rendering both put a 288px `aside` inside a 64px header row. (F17, F18, F19)
- **`anchor` is the truth and `ltp` may be synthetic**, and the split is now enforced by shape as well as by rule: the dashboard's helpers take flat maps of anchors, so there is no `ltp` in scope for a total to read by mistake. The interpolation loop tweens *between* server anchors and invents nothing — an earlier draft specified bounded jitter, which would have put figures on screen that no provider reported. (F19, F21, 3.00.01)
- **Provenance is derived at read time and announced, never merely hovered.** One ticking clock from the layout so the badge cannot contradict the row it summarises; `sr-only` text tied by `aria-describedby` because hover does not exist on touch; only STALE is muted, since muting SIMULATED would grey the whole terminal and stop carrying information. Only symbols actually rendering a price feed the badge — `worstSource([])` is STALE by design, so counting unpriced symbols would pin it there on account of absent data. (F20)
- **A server-rendered price needs server-rendered provenance.** `PriceWithProvenance` renders an em dash whenever provenance is null, so passing `live ? … : null` made every row an em dash in the SSR HTML while the change column beside it showed the server's own figure — a row claiming +25% with no price. Found in the SSR HTML at this checkpoint, not by looking at the screen, where hydration hides it. (F20, fixed 3.00.01)
- **The dashboard aggregates in Postgres through three `security_invoker` views**, none carrying a predicate of its own. Day's P&L was undefined in the contract and became §9. Unpriced holdings are counted and disclosed rather than valued at zero. Tiles jump on the anchor and never tween — `architecture.md` contradicted itself and the invariant won. (F21)
- **The index strip reports a derived composite, never a named index.** NIFTY 50 and BANK NIFTY have no row, quote or anchor anywhere, so the strip carries an equal-weighted mean day change with its constituent count on screen, and its provenance is derived pessimistically. (F17 deferred it, F21 built it)
- **The categorical chart ramp had never been measured and failed when it was.** Two colours were 0.8 apart under deuteranopia — identical to a deuteranope. Hue alone cannot separate ten categories on that axis, so the replacement steps lightness too, and a test now scores every pair under both vision models. (F21)
- **Four defects and one false guarantee came out of the phase review**, all fixed at the checkpoint: the SSR em dash above; two dashboard components subscribing to the whole quote map and re-rendering ~60×/s during every tween (including the Recharts SVG); `seedQuotes` skipping on presence rather than freshness, which pinned a symbol to its first seed for the session if Realtime never delivered; a sign-out menu item that could not be operated by keyboard, because Radix calls `currentTarget.click()` and `HTMLFormElement.click()` has no default action; and a nav-link test that two files claimed existed and neither had written. (3.00.01)
- **The recurring lesson: hydration hides server-render defects.** The em dash bug was invisible on screen and obvious in `fetch()`ed HTML. Its sibling from F20 — a backgrounded tab firing no focus events and running no animation frames — cost time in three separate features. ### Feature 15 — Quote provider chain  *(2026-08-21)*
- Decision: **the simulator walks from a real NSE close.** `library-docs.md` said it seeds "from `instruments` reference data", but that table carried no price — a cold start had nothing to walk from and every symbol would have begun at an invented constant. `instruments.prev_close` is now seeded from the published bhavcopy, found on the **archive** host that F14 already reaches rather than the API that answers 403. MRF opens near ₹1,32,665 and YESBANK near ₹22.80; a shared constant would have priced them identically and made Phase 5's portfolio arithmetic meaningless.
- Decision: **the close is a seed, never a quote.** No `NSE_BHAVCOPY` value joins `quote_provider`, nothing writes it into `quotes`, and no surface renders it as a price. Every price still comes from a row carrying `provider = 'SIMULATOR'` and a null `provider_ts`, which `deriveSource()` renders as SIMULATED. Nullable on purpose: a fresh listing with no close makes the simulator report itself **unavailable** rather than creative.
- Decision: **the seam is built, the limiter is not.** `QuoteProvider`, an ordered chain and a per-provider circuit breaker, exercised against a deliberately failing fake. A token bucket in front of a local simulator caps nothing, so it waits for a provider that makes outbound requests. F14 is why the breaker earns its place now: the failure that mattered was not one bad response but continuing to hammer an upstream that had already said no, which is what kept Yahoo's block alive for forty minutes.

### Feature 11 — Database schema: funds, orders, and portfolio  *(2026-08-21)*
- Decision: **the money tables grant `select` and nothing else.** No client role holds insert, update or delete on any of the six, and no write policy exists — every write arrives through a `security definer` function. `code-standards.md` already forbade a Server Action writing them directly, so a write grant would have existed only to be unused.
- Decision: **three §12 identities encoded as CHECK constraints** — 8 (a non-`OPEN` order holds no margin), 12 (longs hold no collateral and no reference price, shorts carry both) and 6 (`charge_breakdown` sums exactly to `charges`) — alongside all-or-nothing fills, `limit_price` presence, and the zero-quantity rules.

Caught only because the probe was re-run after applying it rather than assumed fixed. Three migrations rather than one, and the history shows why.
- Gotcha: the `orders_limit_price_iff_limit` constraint caught the seed data for the index benchmark — LIMIT rows inserted without a price, intending to set it in a follow-up `UPDATE`. The constraint working on its author is a reasonable first customer.
- Gotcha: dropping `positions_collateral_on_shorts_only` reddens its own assertion *and* the §12.11 positive control further down, because the row the first case now successfully inserts collides on the primary key with the row the control tries to insert. Worth knowing before reading a falsification result as two separate failures.
